description: "Teacher attrition is a key contributor to teacher shortages in many states, particularly those with large geographically rural areas. Using seven waves of the School and Staffing Survey and Kansas as a case study, this study examines the teacher labor force from 1988 to 2012. Moreover, the study describes the teacher mobility patterns for Kansas from 2000 to 2012 and compares them against the national average, the Midwest states, and the Great Plains states. Furthermore, it examines whether younger teachers, teachers with graduate degrees, and STEM and special education teachers in Kansas are more or less likely to turn over. Lastly, the study examines how certain teacher and school characteristics are associated with teacher attrition for Kansas as a whole and for rural schools in particular. Implications for research and policy are discussed."
why_it_matters: "State-level evidence can reveal staffing patterns hidden by national averages. This study examines how Kansas teacher characteristics and turnover differ from national patterns and from nearby rural states."
data_and_sample: "The study uses seven Schools and Staffing Survey waves from 1987–1988 through 2011–2012 and four teacher follow-up waves. Descriptive analyses include 5,100 teacher-year observations; turnover analyses include 2,910 Kansas observations."
key_findings: ["Kansas teachers remained overwhelmingly White while the schools in which they worked became more economically and racially diverse.", "Kansas teachers were more likely to turn over than peers in nearby Midwestern states, with school switching accounting for much of the difference.", "Younger teachers and special educators showed elevated turnover in statewide models; administrative support and union membership were associated with lower turnover."]
policy_implications: ["Use state-specific evidence to target staffing policy rather than assuming nearby rural states face identical conditions.", "Consider workforce diversification and supports for vulnerable teacher groups in Kansas recruitment and retention planning.", "Evaluate improvements in working conditions and administrative support; this study does not establish causal effects of particular retention policies."]
