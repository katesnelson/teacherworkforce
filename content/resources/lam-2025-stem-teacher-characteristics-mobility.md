description: "This study examines the demographics, qualifications, and turnover of STEM teachers in Kansas and Missouri—two contiguous, predominantly rural states in the Midwestern region of the United States. The existing literature lacks detailed insights regarding U.S. STEM teachers, especially with recent economic and social changes over the COVID-19 pandemic, and there is particularly limited evidence regarding STEM teachers in the U.S. Midwest. Utilizing large-scale administrative longitudinal data, we filled part of this gap by documenting the characteristics and turnover patterns of STEM teachers in Kansas and Missouri over a 13-year period, from 2010 through 2023. Our analysis shows declining trends among young and early-career STEM teachers, STEM certification, and rising STEM teacher turnover, especially post-COVID-19. We found particularly high turnover rates in urban schools and schools with the highest shares of students of color and poverty. We also found numerous factors of STEM teacher turnover, including salary and employment in schools serving high percentages of minoritized and low-income students, as well as differential turnover patterns among school geographical circumstances. This work is the first comprehensive examination of STEM teachers in Kansas and Missouri. We offer insights into the teacher workforce of the traditionally overlooked U.S. Midwest. Our results suggest important policy implications for sustaining a diverse and qualified STEM teacher workforce in the U.S. amid post-COVID-19 social changes, thereby informing decision making at state and national levels that aim to foster equitable access to high-quality STEM education among students in diverse contexts, while contributing to the U.S.'s long-term economic growth, sustainability, and the world's advancement of STEM education."
why_it_matters: "STEM staffing challenges differ across school communities, even in neighboring states. Evidence from Kansas (KS) and Missouri (MO) shows why recruitment, retention, and compensation policies should account for local conditions to improve access to diverse, qualified STEM teachers."
data_and_sample: "The study includes all secondary STEM teachers in Kansas and Missouri public schools from 2010–2011 through 2022–2023. Its descriptive sample includes 227,380 teacher-year observations."
key_findings: ["STEM teachers were predominantly White in KS and MO: about 6% were teachers of color across the study period, while the share of students of color in their schools rose from about 25% to 31%.", "The shares of young, early-career, and STEM-certified teachers declined, while inflation-adjusted salaries fell, particularly in the later years of the study.", "Annual turnover was about 13%–15% through 2020–2021 and rose to almost 20% in 2021–2022. Urban schools consistently had the highest descriptive turnover rates.", "Urban schools and schools serving the highest shares of marginalized students employed the least experienced and credentialed STEM workforces; rural schools had the least diverse STEM teachers.", "After statistical adjustments, higher student-of-color shares predicted greater turnover within urban areas, while higher poverty predicted greater turnover within rural/town areas."]
policy_implications: ["Recruit and retain a more diverse, qualified STEM workforce, with attention to declining shares of novice and STEM-certified teachers.", "Tailor policies and resources to local conditions. Different patterns across states, school locales, and student populations suggest that uniform approaches may overlook schools with the greatest staffing challenges.", "Evaluate compensation reforms against local labor-market conditions, including competing STEM careers. Higher pay was associated with lower turnover, but whether salary increases or differentiated pay improve retention requires causal evaluation."]
