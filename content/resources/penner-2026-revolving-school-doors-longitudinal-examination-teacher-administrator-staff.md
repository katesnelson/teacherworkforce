description: "Non-teaching staff comprise over half of all school employees, and their turnover may be consequential for school operation, culture, and student success, yet we lack evidence documenting their attrition. We use 17 years (2007–2023) of administrative data from Oregon to examine mobility and exit among teachers, administrators, paraprofessionals, licensed staff, and non-licensed staff. Although teacher turnover dominates turnover conversations, teachers are consistently the most stable employee group, even as turnover surged following the COVID-19 pandemic. School factors, such as colleague experience, predict turnover rates for all employees, but within-school turnover between staff groups is not highly correlated, and associations with some school context factors vary by employee groups. Results suggest that employee turnover is not a homogeneous phenomenon across staffing groups."
why_it_matters: "Schools depend on many adults beyond teachers, yet staff turnover is rarely examined across roles. This study compares mobility and exit across Oregon’s public school workforce, showing why teacher turnover alone gives an incomplete picture of school staffing instability."
data_and_sample: "The study uses Oregon administrative records covering all public school employees from 2006–2007 through 2022–2023, totaling 1,273,002 person-year observations. School-context analyses use 962,738 observations of school-based employees in schools with at least five employees."
key_findings: ["Teachers were the most stable of the five broad employee groups, with average annual turnover of 17.2% over the study period. Turnover includes school or district moves and exits from Oregon public schools.", "By 2021–2022, turnover reached study-period highs for teachers (21.3%), administrators (26.1%), paraprofessionals (30.7%), and licensed staff (25.9%).", "Teacher retention in Oregon public schools was relatively stable over longer horizons, while paraprofessional and non-licensed staff retention was substantially lower and declined over time.", "Within-school turnover across employee groups was only modestly correlated. School-context patterns differed by role, and some relationships changed after accounting for other staff and school characteristics."]
