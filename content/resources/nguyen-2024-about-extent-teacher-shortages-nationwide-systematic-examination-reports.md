description: "Teachers are critical to student learning, but adequately staffing classrooms has been challenging in many parts of the country. Though teacher shortages are widely reported, they are poorly understood, particularly in terms of the magnitude and variation of the shortages, and reported figures are often localized and anecdotal. To address this gap, we systematically examine news reports, Department of Education data, and publicly available information on teacher shortages for every U.S. state. We characterize the data landscape and find there are at least 39,700 vacant positions along with at least 288,000 positions being held by underqualified teachers, both of which are conservative estimates of the extent of teacher shortages nationally. We discuss the implications of our findings for improving data systems, including more specific and consistent reporting of shortage, as well as implications for teacher preparation and education policy."
why_it_matters: "Teacher shortages are widely reported, but inconsistent definitions and incomplete data make their scale difficult to assess. This study compiles and verifies state-level evidence on vacancies and underqualification, providing a nationwide baseline and showing why national totals alone cannot guide targeted policy responses."
data_and_sample: "The study searches news reports and public agency records for all 50 states and D.C., with final verification completed in November 2023. It combines state reports, mostly from 2021–2022, with federal data; vacancy counts are available for 37 jurisdictions, with differing years and definitions limiting comparisons."
key_findings: ["The study documents at least 39,700 vacant teaching positions and approximately 288,000 positions filled by underqualified teachers. These are conservative estimates, not a complete count for a single school year.", "No usable vacancy estimate could be found for 14 states, which together enrolled approximately 30.47% of U.S. public school students. Missing data do not establish that vacancies are absent.", "The apparent severity of shortages changes substantially when vacancies are measured relative to student enrollment or the teacher workforce rather than as raw counts.", "Many states with low or unknown vacancy counts have large numbers of underqualified teachers. Vacancy and underqualification measure different staffing problems; credentials do not directly measure teacher effectiveness."]
policy_implications: ["Make school-level teacher qualification data readily and promptly available so policymakers can identify where particular staffing needs exist and target investments.", "Maintain accessible data on job postings and unfilled teaching positions.
